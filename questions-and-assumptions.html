Airport Trip Planner: Assumptions

The requirements leave a lot unsaid. This page lists each gap, what we do know, and how the design deals with it.

IDTopicWhat we knowWhat the design does about itWhy it matters
Q-01What we produceA high-level architecture documentKeep it short enough to walk through in one sittingKeeps the effort in proportion
Q-02Which airportThe shops and restaurants are in an airportStart with one terminal; design so more terminals or airports can be addedDecides what "reachable" means
Q-03Time availableWe get the flight timeUsable time = departure − now − boarding buffer (30 min unless supplied) − walk buffer (15 min unless supplied); all times in UTC; estimates shown as estimates (Time)Avoids false confidence about catching the flight
Q-04Where the traveller isNot suppliedUse location and walking time only when a source provides themYou can't tell what's nearby from keywords
Q-05Is the place openWe get the listing and keywordsUse opening hours and locations when a source provides them; otherwise flag itA good match might be closed or too slow
Q-06SalesRecommendations should consider themStore offers with start and end times in each catalogue version; show only offers live right now and only while the catalogue is fresh; no feed, no offers (Sales)Never invent or show stale promotions
Q-07PreferencesWe get the traveller's chosen preferencesTreat things like dietary needs as hard rules and likes as soft onesA preference and a constraint aren't the same
Q-08Quality of suggestionsKeywords are inconsistentJudge the approach against a set of written examplesLets us assess it without training a model
Q-09Scale and speedNo targets givenValidate the chosen 10-second API timeout, 300-second refresh deadline, 15-minute refresh and 30-minute catalogue freshness assumptions against load and catalogue sizeBacks up the infrastructure choice
Q-10When services failThe services are assumed to existFresh under 30 min, stale with a warning up to 4 h, then no reliable data; fast retries; circuit breaker on the traveller serviceKeeps things useful when something breaks
Q-11PrivacyPreferences and flights can be personalStore the minimum; never send personal flight data to the AI providerShapes the architecture and its trade-offs
Q-12TechnologyPre-built AI is preferred; AWS is the platformUse London, Node.js 24 and Nova Micro as the specified design; validate classification quality against a keyword-only baselineGrounds the choices in the requirements

How an assumption gets settled

Write down the answer, where it came from, and which documents it affects. Keep three things apart: what the existing services supply, what we assume, and what we've checked against an outside source.

Numbers such as a boarding buffer, a response-time target or a monthly cost only appear with a source, or clearly marked as an assumption. No real traveller data is needed.
